# Model card: T1 economic-bad risk at origination

Generated by `make model` (stage `model`, `src/mcarisk/model/`). As of 2026-09-30. METRICS.md v1.3. Every number below is computed from the build; the E8 eval (`evals/reports/latest.md`) adds the ceiling AUC from the simulator's true hazard, which pipeline code may not read.

## Intended use
- Rank **new applications and renewals at the funding date** by the probability that the deal becomes economically bad (METRICS.md §5 **T1**: D19 within 180 Fed ACH business days of funding).
- Support underwriting review queues, pricing discussions and portfolio monitoring of the *mix* of risk being written. It is a decision aid for a human underwriter, not an automated approve / decline rule.

## What NOT to use it for (yet)
- **Not** for automated declines or pricing without a human, and not as a probability of loss in dollars (T1 is a binary event; severity is not modelled).
- **Not** for monitoring open deals after funding: features are frozen at the funding date by design. Use D13 / D14 / D16 servicing metrics for that.
- **Not** as a stable absolute probability across regimes: the training window contains the 2024Q3-2025Q1 deterioration, so levels drift (see Calibration). Re-fit and re-calibrate before use.
- **Not** on deals with no tracker row (Q5 `tracker_missing`): they have no origination features.

- Deals funded after 2026-03-31 (`out_of_window`) are scored but never evaluated (none is mature).
- **Tracker-missing deals (44)** are excluded, not imputed: they have no contract terms, ISO or underwriting fields at all. 33 of them are mature; their T1 economic bad rate is 24.2%.
- Missing underwriting fields (about 6% per field, independently, in the 2025+ tracker) are imputed with the training median, with the indicator `uw_missing`.
- Time in business is harmonized to months: tracker `tib_months` (integer) or scorecard `tib_years` x 12 (1 decimal of a year, so +/- 0.6 months). The model uses log(months / 24).

## Features and leakage guard
- Origination-only (METRICS.md §5): FICO, average monthly revenue, NSFs (90 days), positions, time in business, industry, state, contract terms, ISO, and **burden** = daily equivalent x 21 / average monthly revenue. For renewals: the prior deal's D13 pace, D14 return rate (last 20 attempts) and payment ratio **as of the funding date, with no look-ahead** (returns posting after the funding date are ignored; D17 values, which look up to 2 business days ahead, are never used).
- Never used: tracker `status`, outcomes, metric / target tables, or any payment dated after funding. Tests: perturbing every post-funding ledger row, collections row, tracker status and outcome leaves the features unchanged; the feature SQL is checked for forbidden inputs (`tests/test_model_unit.py`, `tests/test_model_build.py`).
- Logistic inputs: `fico_per50`, `log_tib_24m`, `positions`, `nsf_90d`, `burden_per10pp`, `renewal_healthy_asof`, `renewal_stressed_asof`, `log_term_120`, `uw_missing`.

## Target choice
- **T1 economic** (D19 = defaulted / settled / written off **or refinanced distressed**) at a fixed 180-business-day horizon. The fixed horizon avoids the censoring bias of closed-deals-only bad rates.
- The **naive** D18 target ignores distressed refinances, which are the risk renewals hide. The same logistic specification trained on D18 (`logit_naive`) is shown for contrast:

|---|---|---|
| AUC vs economic T1 | 0.660 | 0.650 |
| AUC vs naive D18 | 0.644 | 0.601 |
| Recall of economic bads in top k | 0.386 | 0.406 |
| Recall of distressed refis in top k | 0.386 | 0.386 |
| Mean risk percentile of distressed refis | 0.621 | 0.651 |
| Mean predicted probability | 0.327 | 0.174 |
| Observed economic bad rate | 0.238 | 0.238 |
| Mean predicted probability, distressed refis | 0.398 | 0.233 |

k = 101 (the number of economic-bad test deals); 44 test deals were refinanced distressed by their horizon. The naive model is trained to treat a distressed refinance as a *good* outcome.
On ranking, the naive model ranks them *higher* (mean risk percentile 0.65 vs 0.62): distressed refinances, modifications and defaults share the same distress drivers, so a D18 model still orders them similarly. The damage is in the **level**: the naive model predicts a mean probability of 17.4% against an observed economic bad rate of 23.8% (economic model 32.7%), and 23.3% vs 39.8% for the distressed refinances themselves. Used for pricing, limits or loss reserves, it would understate economic risk by roughly the share of distressed refinances.

## Calibration
- Calibration slope / intercept: logistic recalibration of the outcome on logit(prediction) (slope 1 / intercept 0 = perfect).
- **`logit` is recalibrated on the validation split** (Platt / logistic recalibration p = sigmoid(a + b logit(p_raw)), a = -0.084, b = 0.724, fit on validation only, never on test). **This method was chosen after the test split had been read** (user-approved 2026-11-04): the uncalibrated model's test slope was 0.69 (validation 0.72). The uncalibrated scores are kept as `logit_uncalibrated` (report-only). Recalibration is monotone, so AUC and lift are unchanged.
- After recalibration: test slope 0.96, intercept -0.51, calibration-in-the-large +0.089. The uncalibrated model was too spread out for later vintages (fit on about 500 deals from the 2024Q3-2025Q1 deterioration). `gbm_isotonic` (isotonic on about 320 validation deals) does not transfer as well. Plot: `build/figures/model_calibration.png`. Re-calibrate on the most recent mature window before any use of the probability level.

- **Burden is modelled with renewal status and term.** Overall, burden looks *inverted* (Part 5): distressed refinances are written at a lower payment, and high-burden deals are short deals that close before distress can show within the horizon. ## T2 survival and T3 relationships
- T2 (time to the D19 event, censored at a good close or 2026-09-30): Kaplan-Meier by renewal status, positions and burden (`survival_km`, `build/figures/survival_km.png`); Cox model on the logistic inputs, n = 1967, events = 496, concordance 0.720 (descriptive, all deals).
- T3 (relationship R9, resolved within 18 months of first funding): population 404 of 453 observable relationships (394 scored; bad rate 24.4%); first-deal logistic AUC train 0.748, temporal test 0.691 (n = 224). **Survivorship caveat:** relationships still open at 18 months (long chains) are not in the population, so T3 under-represents them.

## Limits
- Synthetic book with a known data-generating process; the latent merchant quality that drives much of the risk is not observable at origination, which caps achievable AUC.
- Small samples: about 500 training deals; coefficient CIs are wide.
- T1 counts a deal still open at its horizon as good.
- The D17 stress rule used as a renewal feature is evaluated without look-ahead, so it is a slightly weaker signal than the D17 label itself.
- GBM hyperparameters are fixed (`{"learning_rate": 0.05, "max_iter": 150, "max_depth": 3, "min_samples_leaf": 40, "l2_regularization": 1.0, "early_stopping": false, "random_state": 0}`), not tuned.
- Feature table columns: numeric 21, categorical industry, state, iso.
